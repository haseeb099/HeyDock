## Start remotely when you can, and treat a visit as a confirmed event

Remote IT support can clear a large share of everyday faults: accounts, applications, and devices that can still be reached. It is also a poor fit for a laptop that will not power on, a switch in a locked cupboard, or a wireless problem that only exists in one room. Choosing well is a matter of symptoms, not of ideology.

A good first question is whether the affected person or system can be reached safely from elsewhere. If the answer is yes, a remote session is usually the right start. It produces information even if it does not produce a complete fix.

A visit should answer a different question: what must be done in the room, by a person with which skills, in which window, and how will they get in? If those are not confirmed, the visit is not booked. It is a hope.

White-label and MSP work follows the same rule. The engineer may wear your brand; they still need a site contact, a parking or reception plan, and a named escalation if the job is larger than expected.

Hardware faults often need both: a remote diagnosis, then an approved repair or replacement, then a visit or a courier. Our IT support, dispatch and break/fix pages are written around that sequence, not around a promise that every ticket will be closed from a desk.
