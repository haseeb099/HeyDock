## Write the must-haves, and ask how you would leave

Buying business software is easy to start and hard to unwind. HR systems, small-business ERP, ticketing, booking and appointment tools all fail in similar ways: the licence is purchased after a charming demonstration, no owner is named, and the spreadsheet remains the truth.

Write must-haves before you see a demo. Include the reports you cannot live without, the other systems that must connect, and the people who will administer it on a Tuesday. If two products cannot be compared against the same list, you are choosing a presenter, not a system.

Name an owner in your organisation. A vendor can implement; they cannot care more than you do after go-live.

Ask about exit. Can you export your data in a form you could actually use? What happens to files, history and permissions? A product that will not let you leave will also not let you bargain later.

Ask about the operational week: who the users call, what a typical change costs, and whether the vendor or you hosts the system. Then decide whether a product is enough, or whether the process is distinctive enough to justify a custom build.
