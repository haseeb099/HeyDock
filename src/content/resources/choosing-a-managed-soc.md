## Ask who is actually awake, and what they are allowed to do

'SOC' is a label that can mean a staffed operations centre, a tool with a daytime analyst, or a dashboard nobody reads. If you are choosing a managed security operations service in the UK, the useful questions are practical.

What signals will be in scope? Identity, email, endpoints and network detections are different. A service that cannot see a source will not detect attacks that live there. Ask for the list, not the adjective.

What hours have you bought for a human? Monitoring can run all day. A night response is a staffing commitment. If the brochure says 24/7, ask whether that is collection, triage, or only a ticket that waits until morning. Ask what happens at 3am if the alert is serious: who is called, and what they may do before they reach you.

How is noise handled? A thousand daily alerts are not a programme. Ask how detections are tuned, and how often you will review them together.

What is the path into incident response? Monitoring without a written next step becomes a forwarded email at the worst moment. If you already have insurers or legal advisers, ask how they are included.

Finally, ask what you must do: access, context, and a person who can approve a containment step. A SOC cannot compensate for an estate nobody will describe. Our security operations page is written with that honesty in mind.
