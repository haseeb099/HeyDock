## Write the constraints before you write the advert

Remote technical hiring goes wrong when the advert is posted before anyone has agreed the hours of overlap, the employment model, or the systems the person will be allowed to reach. A UK company can hire well at a distance, but only if those constraints are treated as part of the brief.

Start with the work. Which meetings must the person attend live? Which systems need a UK IP, a managed device, or a physical token? If the answer is 'almost all of them', you may not have a remote role.

Then choose a model, with advice from someone qualified on tax and employment law. Direct employment, a UK contractor, or an overseas arrangement each has consequences. HeyDock can discuss options in practical terms; we do not give legal or tax advice.

Access is a design problem. A personal laptop with a personal email is not a workplace. Decide the device standard, the identity, and who approves admin rights, before the start date.

Management is the last constraint people forget. Who is the UK contact? How is work handed over at the end of the overlap? What happens when the person is unwell? Remote work fails quietly when those answers are 'we'll see'.

When the constraints are written, a search is straightforward. Our remote talent and global recruitment pages describe how we run that search. The brief you bring is more important than the job title.
