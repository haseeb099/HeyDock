## Pilot first, then wave, and leave a way to raise day-two problems

A rollout is a logistics exercise as much as a technical one. The build must be repeatable, the site must be accessible, and someone must still answer when a user has a problem on day two. This checklist is the one we use in conversations about UK device and office rollouts.

Write down who is in the wave, what they will receive, and what 'done' means for a single user. Include accessories, accounts, and any line-of-business application that has to be there on the morning they start.

Pilot the build with a small, unsympathetic group. If the pilot fails, stop. A wide deployment of a broken image is a very public way to learn.

Confirm access for each site: a room, a contact, parking or reception, and a window that does not collide with a business event. An engineer without a badge or a table will not recover the day.

Plan the old equipment. Collection, wipe, reuse or disposal should be on the page before the first swap, or you will grow a second project in a cupboard.

Finally, name the handover path. Early-life support is part of the rollout, not a favour. Our rollout service is built around that sequence, including the rule that a visit is confirmed, not assumed.
