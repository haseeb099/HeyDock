## Write the plan, then restore something, or admit you have not

Business continuity and disaster recovery are about the work that must continue, the systems that support it, and the practised path back when they fail. HeyDock helps UK organisations write that plan and test the claims they are willing to make. A backup that has never been restored is a rumour.

We prefer a short, used plan to a long one that nobody can find in an incident.

## Who this is for

Teams with untested backups, organisations with a dormant folder labelled 'DR', and leaders who will have to brief people during an outage. Incident response and monitoring sit alongside. Cloud migrations often need this thinking before a wave is cut over.

## What we deliver

Priorities, backup and recovery design or review, a usable plan, a test that matches the claims, and a maintenance habit. Untested recovery times will not be presented as fact.

## How the work runs

Prioritise, design, test, maintain. A tabletop and a real restore answer different questions; we will say which you are getting. If ransomware is a realistic concern, the plan says so.

Use this page when the essential need is to be able to continue and recover, on purpose.

## Practical limits and how to start

Write down how you will keep operating and recover systems after an outage, and test the claims you are prepared to make. A typical engagement begins with a look at which activities and systems actually have to continue, and then backup and recovery design or review. It is usually a good fit for organisations that have backups they have never restored. If that does not sound like your situation, use the related services on this page; it is better to change the front door than to force the wrong one.

It is also worth hearing the limits in ordinary language. We will only state times we have a reason to believe, preferably after a test. An untested number is a hope. No. Backup is necessary and not sufficient. Recovery is a rehearsed path, including people and communications. We would rather lose a piece of work than invent a promise about coverage, timing, certification or savings.
