## Put a careful pair of hands at the rack, under a script

Data centre smart hands is for the moment when a remote team can see the problem but cannot touch the metal: a drive to swap, a cable to reseat, a power cycle that needs eyes on the lights, a check that a link is actually plugged in. HeyDock can attend agreed facilities in the UK to do that physical work, following a script and the building's rules.

This is not a licence to improvise a redesign in the aisle. The value is a person who turns up, is allowed in, does the agreed task, and writes down what they saw.

## Who this is for

Teams with equipment in colocation, MSPs who rarely visit a given facility, and project leads in the middle of a migration. If the site is an ordinary office, use engineer dispatch. If you need a whole rack build, that is a planned project, not a ten-minute smart-hands ticket.

## What we deliver

We confirm access, book the slot, attend, and record the work. Tasks are listed in advance. If the situation differs, the engineer calls the named contact rather than inventing a new plan. Photos are taken only if you and the facility allow them.

Lead times belong to the facility as much as to us. We will not pretend a same-day visit is possible where induction takes a week.

## How the work runs

Authorise, book, attend, record. Identity, escorts and tools allowed through security are part of the brief. After the visit, monitoring or the remote team confirms the service is back, not only that a cable looks seated.

Use this page for colo and similar facilities. Use dispatch for ordinary workplaces, and rollouts when the work is a planned migration rather than a single hands-on task.
