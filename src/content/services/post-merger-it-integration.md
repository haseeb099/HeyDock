## Keep people working on day one, and sequence the hard joins

After a merger or acquisition, IT work is usually underestimated: two identity systems, two email domains, two finance tools, two ways of raising a ticket, and a set of unofficial instructions that started in a hallway. HeyDock helps UK organisations plan and deliver that integration, with a day-one plan that protects operations and a sequence for the joins that can wait.

We will not assume that the buyer’s stack automatically wins. That is a decision with cost and risk attached.

## Who this is for

Leaders around a deal, IT managers who have inherited a second estate, and teams being told to 'just switch email this weekend'. Staffing, Microsoft 365 and architecture work often appear as slices of the plan. We only work within what you are allowed to share before a deal completes.

## What we deliver

A map of the estates, a day-one plan, a sequenced integration or coexistence design, and help through the transition. Decisions that can wait are listed so they do not clog the first weeks.

## How the work runs

Map, day one, sequence, execute. Support is part of the work because people will be confused. If a system should stay independent for a year, we will say so.

Use this page when two organisations have to operate together, and the IT joins need a plan rather than a slogan.

## Practical limits and how to start

Sequence the IT work after a merger or acquisition so people can work on day one, and so the hard joins are planned rather than improvised. A typical engagement begins with a map of both (or several) estates: identity, email, finance, line-of-business, support, and then a day-one plan that keeps people working. It is usually a good fit for leaders who have just signed or are about to sign, and need an IT plan. If that does not sound like your situation, use the related services on this page; it is better to change the front door than to force the wrong one.

It is also worth hearing the limits in ordinary language. No. Forcing a complete join on day one is a common failure. Some systems can coexist for a period. That is a decision, not a default. We will help you compare, including cost and risk, not just familiarity. We would rather lose a piece of work than invent a promise about coverage, timing, certification or savings.
