## Make releases repeatable, and take them off one person's laptop

DevOps, in the sense we mean it here, is the path from a change in the repository to a release you can describe. HeyDock helps UK teams design and implement CI/CD pipelines and the working practices around them: reviews, secrets, environments, and who is allowed to press the button. The aim is a boring, repeatable path, not a showcase of tools.

If the only way to release is a script on one engineer’s machine, you do not have a process. You have a risk.

## Who this is for

Teams with fragile manual deployments, companies that bought a platform and never finished the pipeline, and product leads who want fewer weekend surprises. If you need the wider cloud estate operated, see cloud management. If you need the application itself built, see custom software. This page is about the path to production.

## What we deliver

A map of the current path, a design that prefers your existing tools where they are good enough, implementation, guardrails, and a handover the team can actually use. We will not impose Kubernetes or a fashionable platform without a reason.

Secrets do not belong in chat logs or unencrypted files. We will treat that as a defect, not a style difference.

## How the work runs

Current path, design, build, hand over. We test with a real change. If the team cannot operate the result, the work is not finished.

Use this page when releases need to become a team asset rather than an individual ritual.

## Practical limits and how to start

Make build, test and release repeatable with pipelines and working practices that match your risk, not a cargo-cult toolchain. A typical engagement begins with a review of how you build, test and release today, and then a recommended pipeline design in tools you already use where possible. It is usually a good fit for teams whose release process lives on one laptop. If that does not sound like your situation, use the related services on this page; it is better to change the front door than to force the wrong one.

It is also worth hearing the limits in ordinary language. Not if the current one can do the job. We prefer to deepen what you already have unless it is genuinely in the way. Only if it is justified. Many teams need a boring pipeline more than a new orchestrator. We would rather lose a piece of work than invent a promise about coverage, timing, certification or savings.

Include the location or systems, the timing, and any access, brand or compliance constraint we should not discover later. We will come back with a practical next step, or with a recommendation to start elsewhere. Submitting the form does not create a paid engagement, a booked visit, or a placement.
