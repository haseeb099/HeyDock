## Give day-to-day IT a written owner

Managed IT is useful when you want one party to own agreed monitoring, maintenance and support, instead of a trail of ad hoc favours and unpaid evenings. HeyDock provides managed IT services to UK businesses and, where agreed, to MSPs who want delivery capacity under a clear scope. The point is not a glossy promise of 'zero downtime'. The point is that someone is responsible for the systems you list, during the hours you buy, and that you can see what was done.

A managed service starts with an inventory and a boundary. If a system is not in the scope, we will not pretend it is. If a request needs a project, we will say so rather than burying it in the ticket pile.

## Who this is for

This suits organisations that are tired of unmanaged estates, or that have some internal IT but need a partner for overflow, maintenance or partner-branded delivery. It is less useful if you only want a single visit or a one-off project; those belong on engineer dispatch, rollouts, or consulting.

We work with companies that can grant access, name a decision maker, and meet to review the service. If you cannot spare that, a lighter IT support arrangement may be better to start with.

## What we deliver

The service is defined in writing: devices, servers, cloud tenants, networks, users, hours, and response expectations that we can actually meet. Monitoring and patching happen inside those limits. Support channels are named. Vendors can be coordinated when that is part of the scope. Reviews use ordinary language, not a dashboards-only ritual.

We will not invent an SLA we cannot staff. We will not use unverifiable uptime statistics. Commercial terms and onboarding come before the service is treated as live.

## How the work runs

Discover, propose, onboard, operate. Changes to scope are discussed, not assumed. If a site visit is required, it is arranged through the same relationship, often via engineer dispatch. If you later want a deeper security or cloud workstream, those have their own pages and should be briefed as such.

Managed IT is the umbrella. IT support, helpdesk, monitoring and on-site dispatch are the moving parts you can also buy more narrowly.

A typical engagement begins with a written scope of what is in and out of support, and then monitoring and alerting for agreed systems. It is also worth hearing the limits in ordinary language. A helpdesk can be part of a managed service. Managed IT usually also covers the systems themselves: monitoring, patching and maintenance, not only user tickets. We can discuss extended hours where they can be staffed and written into the scope. We do not imply around-the-clock cover by default. We would rather lose a piece of work than invent a promise about coverage, timing, certification or savings.
