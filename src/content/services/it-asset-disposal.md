## Retire equipment on purpose, with a record of what happened

Old laptops, servers, printers and phones leave a trail if you let them. HeyDock helps UK organisations retire IT equipment with an inventory, an agreed data-handling method, and a documented path for reuse, recycling or destruction. The alternative is a skip, a drawer, or a well-meant donation of disks that were never wiped.

We will not claim a certified process we cannot show. Where a licensed specialist is the right party, we coordinate and pass their records to you.

## Who this is for

Teams at the end of a rollout, companies closing a site, and anyone who has discovered a cupboard of unused kit. This work often sits beside a rollout or a break/fix replacement programme. If you only need a single device diagnosed, start there.

## What we deliver

A list, a chosen path for each class of asset, execution, and a record. Data-handling is the heart of the job: wipe, destroy, or return. Devices that will not power on still need a plan, because storage can survive the chassis.

We do not dump equipment in ordinary waste, and we do not promise a greener story than the vendor can support.

## How the work runs

List, choose, execute, record. Collection logistics and site access are agreed like any other visit. If some devices are still in use, they stay off the list until they are not.

Use this page when equipment is leaving service. Use rollouts when new equipment is arriving. Use security and data-handling if you need the wider policy context.

## Practical limits and how to start

Take equipment out of service, record it, and dispose of or reuse it through an agreed, documented path. A typical engagement begins with an inventory of the assets to be retired, and then agreement of data-handling (wipe, destroy, or return to you). It is usually a good fit for organisations finishing a rollout with a pile of old laptops. If that does not sound like your situation, use the related services on this page; it is better to change the front door than to force the wrong one.

It is also worth hearing the limits in ordinary language. When a specialist vendor is used, their certificates can be passed through. We will not invent a certificate for work we cannot evidence. We can discuss a wipe standard and who performs it. The method should match the sensitivity of the data, not a casual factory reset in the corridor. We would rather lose a piece of work than invent a promise about coverage, timing, certification or savings.
