## Explain the cloud bill, then change only what you approve

FinOps, here, means being able to say where the money went, who owns it, and which abandoned items can be retired without theatre. HeyDock helps UK organisations read AWS, Azure or Google Cloud spend and make practical, approved changes. We will not promise a headline saving before we have seen the bill, and we will not turn off a production resource as a surprise.

The first deliverable is often a picture finance and engineering can both read. If half the spend cannot be attributed, that is the problem to fix first.

## Who this is for

IT and finance leads who cannot explain the bill, teams whose 'temporary' migration spend became permanent, and companies that already bought reservations but still feel blind. Wider IT cost work, including on-premises and contracts, sits on the IT cost optimisation page. This page is about the cloud bill.

## What we deliver

A readable breakdown, a list of likely waste, proposed changes with risk notes, advice on tagging or account structure, and a review after changes so the saving is observed, not assumed. We use tools you already have where they are enough.

We do not sell panic. Some months the right answer is 'the spend matches the work'.

## How the work runs

See, explain, propose, change and review. Access to billing and a conversation with the people who run the workloads are both required. After the first tidy-up, cloud management can keep the habit alive.

Use this page when the essential need is an honest, owned view of cloud cost.

## Practical limits and how to start

Explain and reduce avoidable cloud spend with visible ownership, without promising a fixed percentage saving. A typical engagement begins with a readable picture of where the money is going, and then identification of waste (idle, orphaned, mis-sized or forgotten). It is usually a good fit for finance and IT leads staring at a bill nobody can explain. If that does not sound like your situation, use the related services on this page; it is better to change the front door than to force the wrong one.

It is also worth hearing the limits in ordinary language. We will not quote a percentage before we have seen the bill and the workloads. Some estates are already tight; some are full of abandoned experiments. No. Anything that can affect service is approved. We would rather lose a piece of work than invent a promise about coverage, timing, certification or savings.
