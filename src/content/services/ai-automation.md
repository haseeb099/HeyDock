## Automate a workflow you can measure, and keep a person responsible

AI automation is worth doing when the work is repetitive, the inputs are reasonably consistent, and you can tell a good result from a bad one. HeyDock helps UK organisations design and run that kind of pilot. We will not start with an unbounded 'AI transformation', and we will recommend stopping if the evidence is weak.

A human remains responsible for decisions that matter. The system should have a list of things it must not do, and a path for when it is unsure.

## Who this is for

Teams with known repetitive work, and leaders who have been asked to 'do AI' and want a contained first step. If you need a full custom application, see custom software. If you need staffed chat, see chat support. The existing planning guide on scoping an AI pilot sits in Resources and is a good brief to bring to the conversation.

## What we deliver

Help choosing the workflow, a written bound, a pilot with review, and a go, adjust or stop decision. Data use is explicit. We do not quietly train public models on your information.

## How the work runs

Choose, bound, pilot, decide. Success checks are agreed in advance so a demo cannot hide a poor result. Extension is a new brief, not an informal widening.

Use this page when you want a first, honest AI step on a real workflow.

## Practical limits and how to start

Start with a bounded workflow, a measurable result and a human owner, rather than an open-ended 'AI transformation'. A typical engagement begins with help choosing a workflow that is small enough to learn from, and then a written scope, success check and list of things the system must not do. It is usually a good fit for teams with repetitive work that is well understood. If that does not sound like your situation, use the related services on this page; it is better to change the front door than to force the wrong one.

It is also worth hearing the limits in ordinary language. The aim is to remove repetitive work, not to surprise people out of a job. Who does what after a pilot is your decision, and it should be discussed openly. Not unless you have explicitly agreed a data use. The default is that your data stays in the agreed system. We would rather lose a piece of work than invent a promise about coverage, timing, certification or savings.
