## Draw what you have, agree a target, and leave the pictures where people can find them

Architecture work is how a landscape becomes discussable: what talks to what, who owns it, where identity lives, and what should change first. HeyDock reviews and designs IT architecture for UK organisations at a level that both engineers and budget holders can use. We are not here to install a heavy framework you will abandon.

The first win is often a current-state picture that is true enough to argue about.

## Who this is for

Teams whose landscape lives in one head, organisations about to add another system, and leads preparing a migration or a vendor change. Cloud, custom software and transformation roadmaps often follow from this work.

## What we deliver

Current state, target, gaps, and usable diagrams. Operational concerns (how it will be run) are included, not left as 'TBC'.

We will not hide uncertainty. If a system is unknown, it is marked unknown.

## How the work runs

Capture, target, gap, leave it usable. Workshops are short and frequent rather than a single grand session. The artefacts should survive our departure.

Use this page when the essential need is a truthful map and a target, not a delivery army (yet).

## Practical limits and how to start

Describe the systems you have, the target you want, and the steps between them, in a way a delivery team can follow. A typical engagement begins with a current-state view of systems, data flows and ownership, and then a target architecture at a useful level of detail. It is usually a good fit for teams whose system landscape lives in one person's head. If that does not sound like your situation, use the related services on this page; it is better to change the front door than to force the wrong one.

It is also worth hearing the limits in ordinary language. Only if it helps. We prefer diagrams and principles a team will use, not a methodology for its own sake. Yes. A critique of an existing design is a valid engagement. We would rather lose a piece of work than invent a promise about coverage, timing, certification or savings.

Include the location or systems, the timing, and any access, brand or compliance constraint we should not discover later.
